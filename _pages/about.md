I am a postdoctoral scholar at the University of California, Berkeley, working on mathematical and computational methods for scientific computing. I completed a Ph.D. in Chemical Engineering and an M.A. in Mathematics at UC Berkeley in 2025.

My research currently spans two principal areas. In continuum mechanics, I study curved and deforming lipid membranes, including their mechanics, permeability, thermodynamics, and coupling to surrounding fluids. In quantum scientific computing, I develop algorithms and explicit circuit constructions for discretized partial differential equations and high-dimensional dynamics. These areas are distinct, but both require moving carefully between mathematical models, computational representations, and the evidence needed to justify a result.

Alongside that research, I build scientific software and tools for mathematical communication. Current projects include finite-element and thermodynamics software, a compiler-like workflow for explanatory Manim videos, and a public workshop on agentic engineering. By agentic engineering, I mean the design of environments in which AI agents can carry out substantial technical work reliably: decomposing objectives into testable tasks, supplying the necessary context and tools, and using specifications, tests, and review procedures to evaluate what they produce. I am particularly interested in workflows that make assumptions, provenance, validation, and the limits of computational claims visible rather than treating them as afterthoughts.

Before Berkeley, I completed B.Sc. degrees in Chemical Engineering and in Pure and Applied Mathematics at the University of Calgary in 2019. Earlier research and engineering placements included polymer-process modelling and bioprocessing. My graduate support included an NSERC Postgraduate Scholarship—Doctoral.
